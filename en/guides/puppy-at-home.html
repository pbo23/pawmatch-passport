A puppy at home: the checklist for the first weeks

6 min readUpdated on 14 October 2026 By the PaWow editors

Bringing a puppy home is exciting, and the first days fly by. With this list you will have everything ready before your puppy arrives, and you will know what to arrange in the first weeks.

Before your puppy arrives: the supplies

A place of their own: crate or bed

A crate gives your puppy a safe place to sleep and settle down, and it helps with house training: dogs don't like to pee where they sleep. Choose a crate in which your dog can stand, turn around and lie down as an adult; a divider makes it smaller while your puppy is still small. Put a cushion or blanket inside and place it somewhere quiet where your puppy can still see you.

Food and water bowls

Choose stainless steel or ceramic bowls: they are sturdy, don't tip over easily and are easy to keep clean. Always have fresh water out.

Puppy food

Ask the breeder or shelter which food your puppy eats now and stick to it for the first weeks: the move is exciting enough. If you want to switch, do it gradually over about a week, mixing a little more new food into the old each day. Choose food made for puppies that suits the size your dog will grow to.

Collar or harness, leash and ID tag

Your puppy grows fast in the first months, so a light, adjustable harness or collar is handy. A leash of 1.5 to 2 metres works well for daily walks. Attach a tag with your phone number to the collar.

Toys and things to chew

Puppies change their teeth between roughly three and seven months and then want to chew on everything. Sturdy chew toys that suit their size will save your shoes and chair legs. Rotate the toys to keep them interesting.

Handy to have at home

  • Poop bags, for every walk.
  • An enzyme cleaner: it really breaks down the smell of an accident, so your puppy doesn't return to the same spot.
  • Small, soft treats for training.
  • A brush or comb that suits the coat of the breed.
  • A chew or lick mat for the moments your puppy has to be alone for a while.

Puppy-proof your home

A puppy explores the world with its mouth. Put away cables, shoes, medicines and cleaning products, and fit a gate at the stairs. Also watch what is within reach: chocolate, grapes and raisins, onion and xylitol (a sweetener found in sugar-free chewing gum, among other things) are toxic to dogs. Some house and garden plants are toxic too; look it up if you are not sure.

The first weeks: what to arrange

Microchip and registration in your name

In the Netherlands every dog must be microchipped and registered within 7 weeks of birth, so your puppy will already have a chip when you pick it up. It should also have a European pet passport. When you collect your puppy, check that the chip number in the passport matches. Then report online that you are the new owner.

Meet the vet

Plan a first visit within the first week or two. The vet checks your puppy's health and discusses the vaccination schedule and deworming. Often your puppy has already had a first vaccination at the breeder or shelter; it will then be recorded in the passport.

Dog tax

Many municipalities charge a dog tax (hondenbelasting). How much it is and how you register your dog differs per municipality; you will find it on your municipality's website.

Consider insurance

Vet treatment can get expensive. See whether pet insurance suits you, and check carefully what is and isn't covered, such as hereditary conditions and the deductible.

Puppy class and socialisation

In the first months of life your puppy gets used to new people, animals, sounds and places most easily. Let it experience lots of different things calmly and in small steps. A puppy class helps with this, and you will learn the basics: sit, down, come and walking nicely on the leash.

House training

Take your puppy outside after waking up, after eating, after playing and otherwise every few hours. Reward it straight away when it pees outside. If it goes wrong indoors, clean up without getting angry: punishment backfires.

Tip: add the chip number, vaccinations and vet appointments to PaWow. That way you have everything at hand, even when the passport is at home, and you get a reminder when the next vaccination is due.
