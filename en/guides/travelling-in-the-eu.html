Travelling with your dog or cat in the EU

5 min readUpdated on 14 October 2026 By the PaWow editors

Taking your dog or cat on holiday in Europe? Within the EU the same basic rules for pets apply. Do start in time, though: some steps take at least three weeks.

The three basic requirements

1. A microchip

Your pet must be microchipped before or at the same time as the rabies vaccination. A rabies vaccination given before the chip was implanted does not count for travel.

2. A valid rabies vaccination

The first rabies vaccination can be given from 12 weeks of age. After that you wait at least 21 days before you can cross the border with your pet. Have the vaccination renewed in time: once it has expired, the new shot counts as a first vaccination again and the waiting period starts over.

3. A European pet passport

The passport contains, among other things, the chip number and the rabies vaccination, filled in by the vet. Always take it with you when you travel: you must be able to show it at a check.

Extra requirements per country

Travelling with your dog to Ireland, Finland, Malta, Norway or Northern Ireland? Then a vet must treat it against the tapeworm Echinococcus multilocularis 1 to 5 days (24 to 120 hours) before arrival. The vet records the treatment in the passport.

Countries outside the EU often have different rules, and the return trip to the Netherlands can have stricter requirements than the outbound one. Check your destination on the website of the NVWA (in Dutch).

How to plan it

  1. A few months aheadCheck that the chip can be read, that the passport is complete and until when the rabies vaccination is valid.
  2. At least 21 days aheadThe first rabies vaccination, if your pet doesn't have one yet.
  3. 1 to 5 days before arrivalThe tapeworm treatment, if your destination requires it.
  4. When you leavePassport, leash, water, food and water bowls, poop bags, any medication and something that smells of home.

On the road by car

Keep your pet secured: in a crate or with a dog seat belt. Plan a break every two to three hours to drink, pee and stretch the legs. Never leave your pet in a parked car when it is warm: within minutes it can get dangerously hot inside.

Seeing a vet abroad

Need a vet along the way? It helps a lot to have your pet's medical history at hand.

Tip: with the QR vet passport in PaWow you can show a vet abroad your pet's vaccinations, chip number and past visits in seconds, in English. The official European pet passport is still required.

This article gives general information; rules can change. Shortly before you leave, check the current requirements with the NVWA and the Rijksoverheid (both in Dutch).
